A BAS is sitting in your ATO account, and the due date is approaching. You have the sales and purchase records, but you'd rather lodge it yourself than pay someone simply to transfer the numbers.

That's possible. The important distinction is between **preparing a reconciled workpaper** and **submitting the activity statement that the ATO has actually issued to your business**.

<h2 id="check-what-ato-issued">Start with the BAS the ATO issued</h2>

For an Australian business, the ATO normally makes the activity statement available before the reporting period ends. Check the activity statement in the relevant ATO online service. The required fields, reporting period and due date depend on your registrations and reporting arrangements.

A sole trader can generally use ATO online services through myGov linked to the ATO. Businesses can generally lodge through Online services for business, which uses a Digital ID and relationship authorisation. The ATO also supports registered agents and approved software channels. Tammy's web tools do **not** connect to these services or submit returns.

<Callout tone="method" title="Do not invent a BAS that the ATO hasn't issued">

If your business isn't registered for a particular obligation, don't assume you should report it because an online calculator displays a label. Check the actual obligations shown in your ATO account.

</Callout>

<h2 id="prepare-gst-figures">Prepare G1, 1A and 1B from reviewed records</h2>

Eligible smaller businesses commonly report using **Simpler BAS**: G1 total sales, 1A GST on sales and 1B eligible GST credits on purchases. Other obligations, including PAYG instalments or withholding, may also appear. Your reporting method and cash versus non-cash accounting basis determine which transactions belong in the period.

Your real numbers can differ because transactions are GST-free, input-taxed, partly creditable, adjusted, or attributed to another period. Supporting invoices and reconciliations matter more than whether a three-field calculator balances. The ATO also instructs businesses to enter whole dollar amounts without cents or rounding up to the next dollar.

<h2 id="lodge-through-ato">Review, lodge and record the confirmation</h2>

Sign in to your actual ATO account, choose the available activity statement, select Prepare, review every displayed obligation and enter the reviewed figures at the correct labels. Check the net amount and any warnings. Submit through the ATO's own Lodge step, then retain the acknowledgement and a copy of the submitted statement.

The [Australian Government BAS guide](https://business.gov.au/finance/tax/business-activity-statement) explains the available lodgement channels. The [ATO's online activity-statement guidance](https://www.ato.gov.au/api/public/content/0-d8324851-ae58-4663-a160-847871068f50) confirms that businesses can lodge and revise most types online and receive a confirmation.

The payment step is separate from calculating or submitting a statement. Check the amount, ATO payment references and your account status; a positive GST difference in an example is not the whole BAS liability.

<h2 id="keep-your-evidence">Keep the workpaper with the evidence</h2>

After lodgement, retain the records behind G1, 1A and 1B, the accounting basis used, any adjustments, reconciled transactions, and the confirmation from the ATO. If a later mistake is discovered, the ATO's rules determine whether an adjustment can go in a later BAS or the original statement must be revised.

Tammy's free workpaper is a preparation aid only. It doesn't establish GST-credit eligibility or your actual lodgement deadline.
