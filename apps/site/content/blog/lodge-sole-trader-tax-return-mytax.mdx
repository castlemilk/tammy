A sole trader with an ABN might have business receipts, employment income and bank interest in the same year. At tax time, those don't become three separate personal tax returns.

In Australia, a sole trader generally reports business income and allowable business expenses **within their individual income tax return**. That is different from a company lodging its own company tax return, and different again from reporting GST on a BAS.

<h2 id="collect-year-records">Collect the records for the correct income year</h2>

Start with the financial year you intend to lodge. Reconcile your invoices and other business income to the bank movements and supporting records. Check expenses, receipts, private-use apportionments, assets and depreciation, stock where relevant, and any prior business loss issues.

For a sole trader registered for GST, ensure your income and expense records reflect the appropriate income-tax treatment of GST. Don't automatically use GST-inclusive receipts as deductible expenses if you were entitled to claim GST credits.

<WorkedExample title="A simplified sole-trader income example">

Imagine $60,000 of business income excluding GST and $24,000 of ordinary deductible business expenses, also excluding claimable GST. The simple difference is $36,000 before other adjustments, losses and personal tax calculations. This is **not** an estimate of how much income tax the person owes.

</WorkedExample>

<h2 id="use-mytax-business-items">Select the sole-trader items in myTax</h2>

Access the ATO through your linked myGov account, open myTax for the intended income year, and check the personal details and prefilled information. The ATO instructs sole traders to select **“You were a sole trader or had business income or losses, partnership or trust distributions (not from a managed fund)”** and then complete the **Business/Sole trader income or loss** section where relevant.

The wording of screens may change. Follow the actual ATO return for your circumstances, rather than copying every field from a blog. Also review applicable salary, wages, investment income, offsets, deductions and tax-prepaid instalments, without counting business amounts twice.

<Callout tone="caution" title="A BAS does not replace your income-tax return">

A BAS reports particular tax obligations, often GST or PAYG instalments. An individual's income-tax return deals with assessable income, deductions and other personal-tax calculations for the financial year. Reconcile both, but do not copy BAS G1 directly into myTax as if it were automatically taxable income.

</Callout>

<h2 id="review-before-lodge">Review the return before you choose Lodge</h2>

The right questions are practical: Are all income sources accounted for? Are deduction claims supported by records and correctly apportioned? Are business assets, losses or PAYG instalments treated appropriately? Do the bank records and business figures tell a coherent story?

Review the complete myTax return, keep the final saved information and use the ATO's Lodge action. Retain the lodgement confirmation and later notice of assessment. The user's own ATO account, not Tammy, provides the official submission result.

A company director preparing a proprietary company's return has a different task; this guide is deliberately scoped to a sole trader's individual return.

<h2 id="plan-next-year">Leave next year's records in better shape</h2>

A return gets easier when source documents, business use, invoices and corrections are recorded throughout the year. Preserve both the original records and the calculations leading to declared figures. Use the [Tammy records guide](/blog/business-receipts-five-year-record-keeping) for retention considerations.
