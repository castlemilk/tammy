The BAS is ready, but the calendar says the deadline is a Sunday. A search result gives you another date, and someone says online lodgement gets two extra weeks.

Don't choose a date by averaging the answers. The actual deadline depends on **your reporting period, lodgement method, and the due date displayed by the ATO**.

<h2 id="the-four-quarters">Start with the standard quarterly reference dates</h2>

For a standard quarterly BAS, the ATO's general dates are 28 October, 28 February, 28 April and 28 July for the corresponding quarters. For a financial year beginning July 2026, the periods fall as follows.

These are the **base reference dates**, not a claim that everyone must lodge on exactly those days. For example, 28 February 2027 falls on a Sunday, and the ATO's next-business-day rule can affect deadlines. The business's actual activity statement or ATO account is the authoritative place to confirm the final date.

<h2 id="extensions-are-conditional">Online and agent extensions are not automatic for everyone</h2>

Quarterly online lodgement may qualify for additional time for the first, third and fourth quarters. The second quarter already has a longer period to February, so the usual extra fortnight for those other quarters does not apply the same way. Eligible registered-agent programs can have different arrangements.

Other activity statements or obligations, including monthly BAS, have different due dates. Monthly GST reporting generally uses the 21st day of the following month, subject to the ATO's specific rules. The standard quarterly table should not be used as a monthly deadline calculator.

<Callout tone="caution" title="Confirm your own due date, not just the calendar table">

Public holidays, weekends, agent arrangements and special circumstances can affect the date you see. Check the ATO-issued activity statement before scheduling payment or lodgement. This article cannot determine your personal extension eligibility.

</Callout>

<h2 id="plan-cash-separately">Plan the payment and lodgement as separate tasks</h2>

A BAS workpaper can help you estimate the GST component, but other tax obligations may be included in the final activity statement. Once you have reconciled invoices and purchases, plan for the ATO's payment deadline as well as completing the lodgement step.

For example, a fictional Simpler BAS with $2,000 GST on sales and $650 eligible GST credits has a **$1,350 GST difference before other BAS items**. This is not evidence that your business owes exactly $1,350.

<Formula label="Illustrative GST-only workpaper" expression="GST on sales − eligible GST credits" result="$2,000 − $650 = $1,350" />

A useful monthly routine is to review incoming cash, upcoming supplier payments and any GST or PAYG amounts before the due date. Use the [12-week cash flow guide](/blog/the-12-week-cash-flow-view) for a dated forecast rather than assuming money will be available on lodgement day.

<h2 id="check-in-ato">Check the actual statement and retain the confirmation</h2>

The ATO generally provides a BAS in advance of the reporting deadline. Once your records are ready, open the activity statement in Online services for business or your applicable ATO account, verify its date, report the correct labels and submit through the ATO's official channel.

Record the ATO lodgement confirmation separately from payment. If you cannot lodge or pay on time, use the ATO's official assistance and extension information instead of treating a generic web reminder as permission.
