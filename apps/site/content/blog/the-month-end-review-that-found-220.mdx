The bank statement closes at **$15,000**. Your cash ledger closes at **$14,780**.

The difference is only $220, which makes it tempting to create an adjusting entry and move on. That's also how a perfectly ordinary mistake survives into next month's records.

A reconciliation works best when the mismatch is treated as a question, not as an amount that needs to disappear.

<h2 id="find-where-the-difference-begins">Find where the difference begins</h2>

Take a fictional small business reviewing its bank account at month-end. The bank's closing balance is $15,000, while the accounting ledger says $14,780. Both are drawn from the same account and the same closing date.

The ledger is **$220 lower** than the bank. A genuine missing outgoing payment would usually make the ledger too high, not too low. That direction helps narrow the investigation.

<h2 id="trace-the-source-lines">Trace the source lines before posting anything</h2>

You compare the bank statement rows with the ledger transactions. A $220 supplier payment appears once on the statement but twice in the books. The duplicate posting reduced the ledger cash balance by an extra $220.

Check the supplier document, the single genuine bank movement and both bookkeeping records. If the second ledger entry is erroneous, correct it through the accounting system's appropriate reversal or audit process. The cash balance then rises from $14,780 to **$15,000** and agrees with the statement.

In real books, the duplicate may also have affected an expense, a payable or a GST account. The correct reversal depends on what the original entry actually posted. Confirm the other side rather than fixing only the cash account.

<h2 id="check-more-than-bank-balance">A close is more than matching one balance</h2>

The bank account is one checkpoint. A disciplined month-end review can also include unpaid customer invoices, bills that have not yet been settled, supporting documents, unexpected account movements and incomplete journal entries.

The ATO's [record-keeping task guidance](https://smallbusiness.taxsuperandyou.gov.au/record-keeping/when-to-do-record-keeping-and-reporting-tasks) explicitly includes bank reconciliation among regular tasks. [Banking record tips](https://smallbusiness.taxsuperandyou.gov.au/record-keeping/tips-for-banking-records) explain how statement downloads and reconciliations help identify fees, omissions and discrepancies.

The checklist is for the reader's own review. Ticking a box does not reconcile a real account, lodge a BAS or save any work to Tammy.

<h2 id="leave-a-readable-trail">Leave enough evidence for the next review</h2>

Save the statement, document why the difference occurred, link any corrective entry to its original source and keep a list of unresolved items. If the next month's opening balance doesn't match the prior month's reviewed close, you should be able to trace what changed.

Tammy's development workspace supports imported bank-statement files and manual reconciliation review. It does **not** claim to connect to live bank feeds or automatically post adjustments.
